InkGrader — architecture

Grading Pipeline

A submission is OCR'd and persisted synchronously; grading itself runs later, out of process, off a Redis queue — fanned out across students and answers with asyncio.gather, then reported back over a WebSocket. The two diagrams below split at that boundary.

1. Submission → queue

Everything up to the enqueue happens inside the HTTP request. Grading does not.

2. process_grading_job: the fan‑out

One job covers a whole exam. It fans out over every student concurrently, and each student fans out again over their own answers, before the two paths converge on a single Redis publish.

orchestration & infrastructure per‑student concurrency guard (lock + publish)

Worth knowing

  • The advisory lock is a no-op outside Postgres. Local sqlite dev always "acquires" — the dedupe protection only exists where engine.dialect.name == "postgresql".
  • Both gather calls swallow exceptions. return_exceptions=True on both the student-level and answer-level fan-out means one bad LLM call or one broken student doesn't take down the batch — it's logged and skipped.
  • The frontend invalidation is exam-wide, not per-student. Every completion event re-fetches the whole exam-students query, so students finishing close together can trigger redundant refetches.
  • tools.py's rag_tool() is unused. It's not wired into any Groq call in grading_agent.py — don't assume RAG-augmented grading is active.
